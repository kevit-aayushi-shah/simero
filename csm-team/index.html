The ownership rule

If a task is assigned to you, you own it until it is closed.

  • You are responsible for following up. Nobody should have to chase you for an update.
  • Do not wait to be given work. Find gaps in your accounts, bring ideas, and act on them.
  • Escalate early with a clear summary: what the issue is, whose side it is on, and what you have already checked.
  • Keep learning the product. Each month you should be able to handle something you could not handle last month.

Onboarding

Weight 15%

You get full ownership of onboarding for your account, whether it is a Netcore or a Chatomate client. The manager joins only when you escalate.

Netcore onboarding

Find the root cause early and escalate fast

  • Keep follow-ups with the client and Netcore to a minimum by asking for everything you need up front.
  • Decide early whether the issue is on our side or on Netcore's side.
  • Escalate to the manager or the Netcore team as soon as the blocker is clear. Do not let it sit.

Chatomate onboarding

Run the onboarding call on your own

  • Onboard the client through the admin panel and embedded signup.
  • Lead the onboarding call yourself, without the manager on the call.
  • Leave the client with a working account and clear next steps.

Initiatives

Weight 10%

This pillar rewards work nobody asked you to do. Use your own skills to improve an account, the team's process, or the product, and log it.

What counts

  • A new use case or upsell idea pitched to a client
  • A template, checklist, or document the team can reuse
  • A quick fix or automation that saves time
  • A product improvement raised with evidence

How it is measured

  • Number of initiatives logged per month (target ≥ 2)
  • Of those, how many came from you and not from an assignment
  • Quick wins delivered within the same week

Feature knowledge

Weight 10%

Every team member must know every Chatomate feature. When a client asks for something, your answer has to be correct.

When you say "yes, it's available"

It must actually exist and work the way you described. A wrong "yes" counts as a mistake.

When you say "no"

Escalate it to the manager as a possible product gap, with the client's exact requirement.
